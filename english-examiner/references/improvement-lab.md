# 15-Point Improvement Lab

Target: **controlled sophistication**, meaning precise, natural, persuasive English with a clear argument. Not ornament.

## Choosing passages

Pick three to six passages where improvement teaches the most: thesis and topic sentences, a device named without its effect, an undeveloped counterargument, a recurring grammar pattern, a strong idea expressed vaguely. Don't rewrite the whole answer first; diagnose, then improve selectively.

## Three levels

| Level | Purpose | Rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Correct | Fix genuine errors only | Keep the student's meaning, structure and voice |
| 2 · Stronger | Clarity, precision, flow, register | Still recognisably the student's sentence |
| 3 · High-level | What a 13–15-point answer might say here | Natural, not ornate; only claims the text supports; `null` when Level 2 is already the ceiling or more polish wouldn't raise the mark |

For the strongest version, the `explanation` covers:
- `effective`: what makes it work
- `nuance`: what shade of meaning it adds
- `improves`: reasoning, structure, analysis and/or language
- `why_in_context`: why it fits this task and text
- `risks`: for example "too elaborate for a summary" or "only if your next paragraph proves it"

Example (from the brief):
- Original: "The author uses many rhetorical devices to show that the issue is important."
- Correct: "The author uses several rhetorical devices to emphasise the issue's significance."
- Stronger: "Through carefully chosen rhetorical devices, the author underscores the issue's wider social significance."

The third version is more precise only if the analysis then names those devices and shows their social dimension. Otherwise it promises more than the answer delivers. The best formulation always depends on the actual devices and their effects.

## Exercises

Every lab item ends with an `exercise` that practises the skill on **different material** (another sentence, the reference text, or a new mini-prompt), with an optional `answer_key`. Copying the corrected sentence teaches nothing; producing a new one does.

Exercise patterns:
- **Effect chain**: "Here is a device + quote. Write one sentence: technique → example → effect → intention."
- **Thesis upgrade**: "Rewrite this generic topic sentence so it could only belong to this text."
- **Counterargument + rebuttal**: "Develop this one-line objection, then answer it starting with 'Even so,'."
- **Collocation repair**: "Choose the natural collocation: make / do / take a decision …"
- **Register shift**: "Rewrite this informal sentence for a formal summary."
- **Compression**: "Say this 40-word sentence in 20 words without losing content."

## Short drills outside exams

When the student wants practice between exams (or `weakness recommend` shows recurring skills), generate a drill set:
- three to five exercises for one skill, using that skill's patterns above
- one short paragraph task (80–120 words) on a fresh prompt

Grade the paragraph with the same standards. It needs no full assessment record; summarise it in chat. Full exams should still be saved and assessed through the CLI so progress tracking stays meaningful.
